Achaemenid Architecture (c. 550-330 BCE)
Monumental building style of the Persian Empire under rulers such as Cyrus
the Great, Darius I, and Xerxes I. Best known from the ceremonial complex at
Persepolis: a stone terrace reached by monumental staircases carved with
processional reliefs, tall slender columns topped with paired animal
capitals (bulls, griffins), and wide flat timber roofs over columned halls
(apadana). The style blends Mesopotamian brickwork, Egyptian-influenced
cornices, Ionian Greek stone-carving, and Median timber roofing. Conservation
issues include erosion of exposed limestone reliefs, instability of
unsupported freestanding columns, and loss of glazed-brick decoration.
